### New features

- `keeper_sync_project` now syncs a large LTD product across a chain of jobs, called slices, instead of one job that either walked every edition or was cancelled at its timeout. Each slice starts editions only until its budget, `DOCVERSE_KEEPER_SYNC_SLICE_BUDGET_SECONDS`, runs out. The budget is checked before each edition and never during a copy, and the `main` edition is always visited first, so a chain's first slice publishes the default edition. A slice that ran out of budget completes its `queue_jobs` row and creates the next slice's row in the same transaction, then enqueues it with a cursor (`resume_after_ltd_edition_id`) and `slice_index` advanced by one. The next slice skips every edition up to the cursor without calling LTD. Because a slice's row is completed in the transaction that creates the next one, the project's active-job slot never looks free mid-chain: the tier crons and a run's discovery skip the project and `POST …/refresh` returns 409 until the chain ends. A run's `total_count` grows by one per continuation, and the run stays `in_progress` until the last slice and then finalises once. Every slice records `slice_index`, `editions_total`, `editions_visited`, `editions_remaining`, `last_visited_ltd_edition_id` and `continued` (plus `continuation_job_id` when it handed off) on its row's `progress`. A slice that ran out of budget before visiting any edition does not continue: it completes `completed_with_errors` with `reason: no_progress` and logs a warning, so a chain cannot loop. This unblocks syncing `pipelines` (2,932 LTD editions and 3,455 builds).
- The budget has no literal default. It derives to `DOCVERSE_KEEPER_SYNC_JOB_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` less a 600 s margin (`KEEPER_SYNC_SLICE_MARGIN_SECONDS`), so 3000 s at the stock 3600 s timeout, floored at a third of the timeout for very short test timeouts. An explicit value overrides the derivation and must be greater than 0 and less than the timeout, or configuration fails at startup. The sync worker logs the budget, timeout and reaper threshold it runs with at startup as `Keeper-sync time ladder`. The Phalanx value that sets the budget comes in a separate Phalanx change.

### Bug fixes

- A queue job that arq cancels, at its timeout or because the worker is shutting down, now fails its own `queue_jobs` row and rolls up its run, instead of leaving the row `in_progress` until a reaper finds it. On roundtable-prod on 2026-09-24, four `keeper_sync_project` jobs cancelled at their 3600 s timeout stayed `in_progress`, and their run blocked new runs for the organization with a 409 for six hours (#699). arq's cancel is an `asyncio.CancelledError`, which the jobs' `except Exception` branches never saw. Every `queue_jobs`-backed worker function now records the cancel from a fresh database session: `build_processing`, `dashboard_build`, `dashboard_sync`, `publish_edition`, `keeper_sync_project`, `keeper_sync_run_discovery`, the three keeper-sync tier crons, `edition_reconcile`, `git_ref_audit`, `lifecycle_eval`, `purgatory_cleanup` and `project_github_resolve`. The row's `errors` carry `type: CancelledError`, `reason` (`job_timeout` when the job ran out its arq timeout, `worker_shutdown` otherwise), `message`, `elapsed_seconds` and `timeout_seconds`. A cancelled `keeper_sync_project` also records how far its slice got. The job's run is rolled up (a keeper-sync run finalises `partial_failure` and publishes `keeper_sync_run_completed`), and what the job's own error path would have failed is failed too: `build_processing`'s build, `dashboard_sync`'s binding, a lost discovery's run. Only a `job_timeout` is captured in Sentry, as `Queue job cancelled at its arq timeout`; a shutdown cancel is routine and is only logged. The cancel is then re-raised, so arq still records the job as failed. The tier crons, `project_github_resolve` and a slice handing off to its continuation also fail any child row a cancel stranded between its commit and its arq enqueue, with the creator's name in `errors.job_function`, instead of leaving it, and the project's active-job slot, to the orphan sweeps. A worker that is OOM-killed still leaves its rows to the reapers.

### Other changes

- `DOCVERSE_KEEPER_SYNC_REAPER_THRESHOLD_SECONDS` can no longer be set above `DOCVERSE_KEEPER_SYNC_JOB_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` plus `KEEPER_SYNC_REAPER_MARGIN_SECONDS` (1800 s). A larger value is capped there, and the sync worker logs `Keeper-sync reaper threshold capped at the job timeout plus margin` at startup with the requested and effective values. At the stock timeout, the 21600 s that the Phalanx chart pins today becomes 5400 s, so a keeper-sync row orphaned by an OOM kill is reaped within two hours of starting, however high the deployment sets the threshold. A value at or below the cap is unchanged. Dropping the Phalanx pin comes in a separate Phalanx change.
- New page, `docs/keeper-sync-budget.md`, linked from `docs/index.md` under Operations. It covers the budget, timeout and reaper threshold ladder and how the three derive from each other, how a chain of slices reads in `GET /orgs/{org}/jobs` and on its run's counters, the cursor rule and the changes it leaves to the tier crons, the cancellation `errors` payload and how to tell a timeout from a deploy, which worker functions record their cancellation, the no-progress guard and raising the timeout as its escape hatch, and the reaper cap. `docs/keeper-sync-scope.md` now says a run's `total_count` grows with its `publish_edition` jobs and continuations. `tests/docs_test.py` fails when the page stops matching the ladder's settings and derived defaults, the progress keys a slice records, the cancellation `errors` payload, the worker functions that record their cancellation, or the log lines it tabulates.
