### Bug fixes

- `keeper_sync_reaper` now sweeps the two keeper-sync families for abandoned queue jobs — a `queue_jobs` row that reached `status='queued'` with a non-null `backend_job_id` and was then lost by arq. `QueueJobStore.fail_abandoned_tier_cron_jobs` covers the run-less tier-cron rows (`kind='keeper_sync_project'`, `keeper_sync_run_id IS NULL`), releasing the `has_active_for_subject` mutex that otherwise parked a project's tier-cron sync forever, and `QueueJobStore.fail_abandoned_run_children` covers run-attributed children, whose reaped rows feed the same `maybe_finalise_run` pass as the silent sweep so an abandoned child stops blocking its parent run. Both age candidates out on `DOCVERSE_KEEPER_SYNC_REAPER_THRESHOLD_SECONDS` (90 min at stock settings) and fail a row only after `QueueBackend.get_job_metadata` confirms arq has no record of it; reaped rows carry `errors.type='AbandonedQueueJob'`, and the reaper's warning line gains `tier_cron_abandoned_count`, `run_attributed_abandoned_count`, and a `reaped_jobs` list naming the matching sweep and `backend_job_id` for every row. An unreachable queue backend aborts only the two abandoned passes for that tick.

- `ArqQueueBackend.get_job_metadata` now consults Docverse's dedicated pool queues (`docverse:sync-queue`, `docverse:maintenance-queue`) before reporting that arq has no record of a job. arq stores a job's definition under a queue-agnostic key but resolves its *status* through the queue's own sorted set, so a job that is merely `queued` on a dedicated pool queue read as "not found" when looked up under the default queue name alone. Since the abandoned sweep treats "no record" as "arq lost this job", the queue-scoped lookup would have reaped every healthy keeper-sync job the moment it aged past the threshold.
