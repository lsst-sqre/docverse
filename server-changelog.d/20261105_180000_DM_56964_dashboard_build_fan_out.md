### Backwards-incompatible changes

- `dashboard_build_reaper_threshold_seconds` must now be at least `dashboard_build_job_timeout_seconds` plus `DASHBOARD_BUILD_REAPER_MARGIN_SECONDS` (900 s, one 15-minute `dashboard_build_reaper` cron gap). If it is not, configuration fails at startup with an error that names all three values. Without the check, a timeout raised past the threshold, or a threshold lowered under the timeout, would let the reaper free a project's rebuild slot while arq is still rendering its dashboard. The stock pair, a 900 s timeout against the 1800 s threshold that Phalanx sets on dev and prod, sits exactly on the floor. So raising the timeout means raising the threshold too, and a non-prod environment that set the threshold to a few seconds to watch the reaper fire must lower the timeout alongside it.

### New features

- `dashboard_build` now runs under an explicit arq timeout that operators can change, `dashboard_build_job_timeout_seconds` (`DOCVERSE_DASHBOARD_BUILD_JOB_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`, default 900 s), instead of arq's implicit 300 s default. A cancelled job's `queue_jobs` row records the configured value as `timeout_seconds`, and its `reason` is read against that value. The Phalanx chart will render it as `config.dashboardBuild.jobTimeoutSeconds` (#873).
- New operations page, `docs/dashboard-build.md`, documents the timeout and reaper threshold side by side, the floor between them, what a timed-out render records and leaves behind, the order the dashboard's files land in, and the upload transport that edition files share with `build_processing`.

### Bug fixes

- Fixes #863: `dashboard_build` was cancelled at arq's 300 s default on every rebuild of the largest projects (`ts-xml` with about 1,200 editions, `ts-observatory-control` with 472). It uploaded one `__editions/{slug}.json` per edition strictly one at a time, at about 1.5 to 2 s per presigned PUT from prod. The landing page, 404 page and switcher still upload first, one at a time and in that order, so they land before any edition file. The edition files now follow concurrently, with at most `keeper_sync_upload_concurrency` (32 by default) in flight. Every PUT goes over the worker's copy client and holds a slot of the worker-wide upload limiter that `build_processing` uses, so the two share one cap on the default pool and no new setting is needed. At the stock cap, a 1,200-edition rebuild now takes about 75 s. The first failed upload cancels the others and fails the job with the object store's own error. The job's `object_count` and `total_size_bytes` are unchanged.
